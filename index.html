01 · THE CITY

The customer that decides whether Voi stays

Oslo gives three operators a two-year permit, with no extension, on the strength of a written bid. It removed an incumbent in 2022, 2023 and 2024, and Voi lost Bergen in 2026 despite being the incumbent. Rider value only counts while the permit lasts, so every initiative is also judged on the award criteria.

Convenience Seekers

Hub & Book

Reserve tonight in a hub zone at a Ruter station or campus; a charged scooter is waiting at 07:45. The bookings become the overnight fleet plan and evidence for the city.

Ops hold a nearby scooter or place one during the 23–05 closure

Social Leisure Seekers

Group package

One price for 2–5 scooters on a shared evening out, with an optional venue partner. Built on Voi's existing Group Rides.

Host buys a package for the group before 23:00
Guests ride on the host's account; new guests are invited to register
Discount only on the package; partner venue co-promotes
Why this segment: riding with friends 6.10 vs 3.83 overall; their top pass benefit is discounted evening and weekend trips; their fallback is taxi.

10 · METHOD

How the engine works

States. Each segment's customers are Acquaintances (A, occasional PAYG), Friends (F, weekly PAYG), Partners (P, Voi Pass) or Lapsed (L). They move between states with monthly probabilities (Johnson & Selnes 2004; Dwyer, Schurr & Oh 1987). Lapsed customers can be won back or leave for good.

Baseline. The starting portfolio is the steady state of the baseline transitions with constant acquisition, so "no initiative" stays flat. The acquisition needed just to stand still is reported (Reinartz, Thomas & Kumar 2005).

Calibration. Oslo revenue is Voi Norway revenue (EUR 19.0m) × Oslo share (derived 60–85%). Voi's Oslo trips are Ruter's 20.4m trips × Voi's trip share (36.6% in 2024). Their ratio is revenue per ride. The pass fee is the list price net of 12% VAT, and PAYG revenue absorbs the rest. The implied monthly-pass revenue share (~30%) is a check against Voi's 38.8% (incl. day passes) and Lime's 28%.

Segments. Segment engagement is calibrated to the share of weekly-or-more riders in each cluster, shrunk toward the pooled share because clusters hold 13–22 users. Rides per state come from Q1 band midpoints.

Value. CPE is the discounted monthly contribution of all customers over the horizon, minus setup (Berger & Nasr 1998; Gupta, Lehmann & Stuart 2004). Initiatives are judged by ΔCPE versus the baseline.

Licence. Oslo's permit lasts two years with no extension. After it ends, each month's contribution counts only with the probability that the city renews it. That gives a rider part and a licence part of ΔCPE. Renewal chances are judgements; Oslo publishes criteria, not scores.

10,000 pilots. A Monte Carlo draws the uncertain inputs from triangular ranges. At the gate the pilot measures behaviour with error and continues an initiative only if its remaining value is positive, so sunk setup is ignored. The value of the gate is the gain in expected ΔCPE over launching blind.

Hub & Book. A placement costs field labour (SSB wages incl. employer costs) × minutes (OR literature), plus vehicle cost. Only bookings that cannot be served by holding a nearby scooter need one. That is why hub zones matter. Booking included in Pass gives Friends a reason to become Partners. Better placement also helps non-bookers (Kabra et al. 2020).

Group package. Only incremental occasions earn margin and create guest accounts. The discount on occasions that would have happened anyway is charged as cannibalisation.

Limits. The survey (n=72, convenience sample, mostly students) supports needs and relative usage, not effect sizes. No public e-scooter retention data exists; transitions are calibrated to the survey mix and to bike-share attrition. The JavaScript engine is checked against an independent Python implementation.
